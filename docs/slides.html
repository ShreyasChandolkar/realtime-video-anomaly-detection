Pipeline

Why this shape

  • Frozen encoder, one pass. Features computed once, read three ways — why it beats realtime on a modest GPU.
  • Prompts are data, not weights. A twelfth class is a sentence, not a retrain.
  • Score against normal. Margin over the best of ~70 normal prompts; raw similarity just describes the scene.
  • Long video needs a baseline. D3 keys on the rise over a causal 20 s EWMA, so a busy junction isn't permanently anomalous.
  • Locating ≠ naming. Detector says when, probe says what — 63% vs 33% on class.

The two failures that taught us the most

  • Run 3 (D3 halved). We shrank intervals to their peak width. D3's real events run 38–125 s, so a tidy span misses them entirely. Lesson: cap the count, don't trim the extent — which became run 8.
  • Run 5 (D2 collapsed). Forcing every video to fire cost 8.7 ≈ exactly one video (8.75). That accident revealed run 7: the head's silence on normal footage was worth more than its localisation.

What actually found the bugs

  • Looking at the frames. One labelled contact sheet of every predicted event. Ten minutes, four real defects.
  • A clip called wrong_way_driving ×6 — it was queued trucks on the far carriageway.
  • A dashcam crash compilation typed stalled_vehicle — watermark visible in shot.
  • Run 9 then flipped fire↔smoke on exactly the two clips we'd flagged by eye, from the prompt bank alone.
