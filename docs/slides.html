Pipeline

Why this shape

  • Frozen encoder, one pass. Features computed once, read three ways — why it beats realtime on a modest GPU.
  • Prompts are data, not weights. A twelfth class is a sentence, not a retrain.
  • Score against normal. Margin over the best of ~70 normal prompts; raw similarity just describes the scene.
  • Long video needs a baseline. D3 keys on the rise over a causal 20 s EWMA, so a busy junction isn't permanently anomalous.
  • Locating ≠ naming. Detector says when, probe says what — 63% vs 33% on class.

Plus a reasoning bonus of +3.5/5 throughout — 57.7 total. The benchmark changed mid-event: run 9 scored 55.3 before the penalty and 50.9 after, unchanged. Any delta measured across that line is meaningless.

What worked

  • Run 7, the biggest single gain. The head matched 0 of 12 events but correctly stayed silent on the one normal video; the onset localised well but fired on it. Chaining them collected both — 14.0 → 21.3.
  • Runs 2 and 9, both on D1. One clip read normal 0.132 vs smoke 0.131. Letting a second component break ties like that took D1 46.4% → 64.1%.

What didn't

  • Run 3 halved D3. We tightened intervals to their peak width; the real events run 38–125 s, so tidy spans miss them under tIoU 0.5.
  • Run 5 cost a whole video. Forcing every clip to fire lost 8.7 marks — almost exactly the 8.75 of the one normal video.
  • D2 and D3 want opposite things. Cutting events helped D3 (+3.3) and hurt D2 (−0.5), so no single global rule fits both.

The pattern: every gain came from a routing rule — which component may speak, and when. Every loss came from tuning a number to the data. The three winning changes are about 30 lines of code, no new weights.
